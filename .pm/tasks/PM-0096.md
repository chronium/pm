## Goal

Refine the activation switchboard so trigger state, requirement progress, provenance, consumers, and available actions are easy to scan in the real settings surface without sacrificing task-board density.

## Problem

PM-0092 established the complete functional switchboard, but its first-pass presentation is deliberately utilitarian. The information hierarchy, expanded-row composition, responsive behavior, and relationship between status and control-plane actions need a dedicated design pass rather than accumulating one-off CSS fixes.

## Scope

- Evaluate the switchboard inside the full Settings page in light and dark themes, not only in an isolated Storybook canvas.
- Refine the collapsed row hierarchy so title, key, activation state, and requirement progress scan clearly.
- Refine the expanded requirements, activation provenance, consuming milestones, and contextual actions as one coherent surface.
- Use existing PM surfaces, spacing, typography, buttons, focus treatment, and semantic color tokens; avoid harsh or stacked borders, excessive cards, shadows, and ornamental styling.
- Preserve dense layouts and handle long trigger names, many requirements, many consumers, and long override reasons.
- Cover desktop, narrow desktop, and mobile layouts without hiding essential state or depending on hover.
- Keep manual, automatic, override, latched, pending, reconciliation-required, read-only, empty, loading, and error states visually distinct and accessible.

## Acceptance criteria

- The trigger's current activation state and requirement progress are the first information visible when scanning each row.
- Secondary identifiers and provenance remain available without competing with the primary state.
- Expanded content has a clear reading order and aligned actions with no double borders, edge crowding, or unnecessary nested surfaces.
- Keyboard navigation, visible focus, touch targets, accessible names, and reduced-motion behavior remain correct.
- The design works at realistic data density in both themes and at mobile width.
- Storybook stories demonstrate the important state combinations and responsive layouts.
- Focused component and browser coverage protects the refined interaction and layout behavior.
